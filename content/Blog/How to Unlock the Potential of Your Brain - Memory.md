This is the first of a series I'm starting focused on how we can take full advantage of the most remarkable organ in our body: **The Brain**. Today's issue will focus on the remarkable and often misused function of our brain, our **Memory**.

## Memory: Our doorway to the past

We can say with certainty our life experience, all the good things and challenges we had to pass through has made us who we are. I can't imagine the person I'd be if I didn't go to Uni or went to a different high school. A large portion of our personality is based on our experiences and our memory of them. So if memory plays such a large role in our lives and on who we are, **what is it?**

According to Wikipedia, memory is

> **Memory** is the faculty of the mind by which data or information is encoded, stored, and retrieved when needed. It is the retention of information over time for the purpose of influencing future action.

This makes it sound like our memory stores factual information, and it can. It can also store a version of the truth or a completely fabricated scenario we think happened. Before we talk about how to wrangle this tool of ours, let me take you through the basics.

## Long Tern Vs Short Term

We often hear of the two types of memory, long term memory and short term memory. In short, **long term memory** is when we commit some information that we will recall after a significant amount of time, like more than a few hours later. **Short term memory**, on the other hand, is the information we keep in our mind to understand the context of what we are doing in the moment. On average, the human brain can keep anywhere between 3 to 7 bits of information at any given moment.

Depending on the emotional state we are when something happens and the amount of attention we are paying, information is stored into the Long Term memory. All information committed to the Long Term Memory is not equally stored as some memories are near unforgettable while others are forgotten in just days.

## How our memory lies

As I said, the strength of a memory depends on our emotional state and the attention we were paying while experiencing that event or information. Aside from not committing the correct information to memory, our emotional state towards the thing we are remembering also affects how we remember something. Let me give you an example.

### Jack and Mary

Let's say Jack and Mary have been dating for a few months. Everything is going and Mary's friend asks her how the two of them met.

If Mary is happy with Jack in that moment and is feeling love when trying to remember, her memory will highlight all the positive things that were happening when the two of them met. Her mind will literally draw sunshine and rainbows into her memory as long as it doesn't break the logical consistency.

If Mary is annoyed or even angry with Jack when trying to remember, her memory will highlight all the negative things that took place when they met. It was cold, his hair was messy, it was raining and other additions to this recalling of the moment to make it more negative.

Now in this example, I am exaggerating to make a point. However, even though our mind might not go to such extremes, it will alter then memory based on your emotional sate at the moment of recalling.

## How to remember everything

The average person's brain filters out about 99% of sensory information it receives from our 5 senses. It does this to not overwhelm it's self when it tries to understand it's environment and give commands to other organs on what they should do. In addition, it also blocks out information that we are not paying attention to. By doing this, we can have a minimized digestible version of the world we can understand and interact with.

If this is the case, how can we remember more than we do now and commit them into long term memory? By using **Memory Techniques**.

### Memory Techniques

The first time I used a memory technique is when I was 12 to remember a list of objects as a part of a memory exercise. I can vouch for these techniques working cause after 15 years, I still remember every object in that list without skipping a beat!

There are lots of memory techniques out there. The most common ones are:

- The Link System
- The Peg System
- The Memory Palace
- Mnemonics
- Chunking

There are too many techniques to mention here but most of them operate on three main principles.

1. They build up on **knowledge you already have**
2. They make you associate the information with something else (usually with something you already know) in a **ridiculous fashion**
3. They make you **pay attention** to the information you're trying to commit to memory

The bottom line is, although it would be very hard to remember everything, you can make sure you remember what you need when you need it by applying this technique.

There are people that'll say "I have a great memory and I don't need any help". If that's the case, good for you! However, the human memory has no upper limit on how much it can improve and how much it can store. Short of you having a photographic memory, you can always improve your ability to recall information.

## Real life applications

Aside from the obvious applications to this new revelation like studying and remembering static important information, it can be integrated into every aspect of your life. Below are some applications I've personally implemented in my life in the past.

- Remembering shopping lists
- Remembering the page number of a book I'm reading (even if I hadn't picked up that book for over a year)
- Remembering phone numbers
- Remembering names and faces
- Remembering the full names and roll numbers of my class mates for roll call

Although it does take an effort implementing these techniques with these applications, it get's exponentially easier with time and more like second nature.

## How to Start Remembering Now

The most straightforward thing you can do to start remembering right now is **Paying Attention**. I know, it sounds too simple to be true, but it is that simple. However, don't think simple means easy as paying attention is a muscle you have to build up as much as your biceps.

If you want a slightly easier path, start with the Link System. It's so easy you can get the fundamentals in less than 5 - 10 min and it forces your attention on the information at hand.
