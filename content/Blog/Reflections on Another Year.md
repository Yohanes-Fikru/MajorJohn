The Earth has completed its 27th orbit around the sun since I was born into this world. This past year has been one of experimentation, perspective shifts, and starting lifelong commitments. I want the coming year to be a year of acceleration, so I thought I’d share some insights I’ve gathered along the way.

# **My Game Dev Journey**

Game development has paradoxically been both my lifelong passion and my biggest source of fear ever since I started doing it professionally. What started as a simple test for a friend’s game turned into a full-fledged career — one that’s both incredibly challenging and deeply rewarding.

Let me tell you about the crucial turning point that made me a “GAME DEVELOPER.” It was the D5 Game Jam. For the uninitiated, a Game Jam is a hackathon-style event where people from different disciplines — programmers, 2D and 3D artists, sound designers, story writers, and experience/game designers — collaborate over a weekend to create a playable game in just 48 hours. Before this, I would take over a week — sometimes two — to prototype an idea. The idea of doing it in just two days? Insane. But here’s what I didn’t account for: I wasn’t alone! I had the expertise and support of 30+ other participants, and to my surprise, we actually finished a playable game. That experience opened my eyes to what teams can accomplish when working toward a shared goal.

Another thing I didn’t realize at the time was the power of a network. The people I met at that Game Jam would go on to recommend me for projects, connect me with jobs, and even become my current collaborators.

After participating in around 18 Game Jams and working professionally on other people’s games for the past six years, my partner and I finally released a game called _Lomi_ under our studio, Muqecha Studios. With two more games on the way, we’re determined to leave our mark on both the African and international game dev scene.

# From the Classroom to the Real World: What It Took to Go Pro

My academic journey was smooth — until university. I was always the kid who got high marks, carried group projects, and put in minimal effort to stay at the top. I assumed university would be the same. I was wrong.

I went to Adama Science and Technology University (ASTU) — not to be confused with Addis Ababa Science and Technology University (AASTU). Although a respected institution, my experience there felt like EATING GLASS. Cooked under the Adama sun, it was both the most boring and the most difficult thing I had ever done. Who knew school could be _that_ horrible? Okay, maybe I’m exaggerating a bit, but to my small, low-attention-span teenage mind, it felt accurate. A mix of bad encounters with certain professors and unfortunate circumstances almost led me to drop out. At one point, I stopped caring about grades altogether — I was just there for my friends and the vibes.

Then came GAME JAMS & Hackathons! These events were where I met like-minded and more experienced people, and where I learned about real-world career paths, landing jobs, and excelling at them. One realization hit me after attending multiple events — the active tech community was much smaller than I expected. If you went to 5 or 6 events, you’d start seeing the same faces. And at that point, even if you weren’t a people person, you’d eventually start talking to someone.

Two things changed my professional life:

1. Feeling like I had failed academically in my second year of university.  
2. Realizing a simple truth: **PEOPLE WORK WITH PEOPLE THEY LIKE**.

Don’t get me wrong — you can’t just coast on connections and expect jobs to fall into your lap. But as long as your skills are solid, you don’t have to be the _best_. You just have to be good enough and build strong relationships. Don’t believe me? I can confidently say that 95% of all the jobs I’ve had since my fourth year in university came through recommendations. My relationships got my foot in the door, giving me the opportunity to showcase my skills in interviews. Ironically, the remaining 5% of jobs only came _after_ I had already built up experience.

# Does What You Learn in the Classroom Apply to the Real World?

Well… **YES, but actually NO.**

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:300/0*tXwl6IP29szeQOYV.jpg)

Especially in software development, what you learn in class is just the _tip_ of the iceberg. Courses provide an introduction, but they barely scratch the surface. This applies even to your final-year coursework. If you want to stand out, you need to go beyond the syllabus, develop genuine curiosity, and put in extra effort.

At a time when _everyone_ is jumping into software engineering, attracted by the promise of remote work and high salaries, you need to be _effective_ in your extra efforts. Simply meeting the minimum requirements won’t cut it.

In freelancing, your network becomes even more important. Referrals are the most powerful way to land projects. A recommendation from a former client or someone you’ve worked with holds more weight than simply applying for a job with the perfect resume. Why? Because, again — **PEOPLE WORK WITH PEOPLE THEY LIKE**. Master that, stay disciplined, and stick to a consistent work schedule, and freelancing success becomes much more attainable.

# Key Takeaways & Advice for the Year Ahead

Looking back, the biggest lesson I’ve learned this past year is this: **No matter the circumstances, I have to PULL THE TRIGGER**.

- **Experience beats theory**. You will learn far more by _doing_ than by just studying.  
- **Networking is everything**. Skills alone aren’t enough — your relationships open doors.  
- **Consistent progress wins**. Even slow progress is better than standing still. Over time, small steps turn into leaps.

This coming year, I want to pour my passion into everything I do. I also want to push myself to create and share more content — because communication in the digital age is a skill that will take me to the next level.

If you’re on a similar path, my advice is simple: **KEEP MOVING FORWARD**. No matter how small, progress is still progress. And as you grow, you’ll learn to progress faster and take bigger leaps in all aspects of life.
