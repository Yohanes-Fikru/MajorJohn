Ever met someone who seems naturally brilliant? Or someone who started off average but became a powerhouse?

Today we will discuss about the age old question, if humans are born smart or made smart. Our goal is to understand what makes our minds develop, and to know definitively how much we can push our minds to the brink.

## Nature: The Case for Being Born Smart

When discussing about nature given traits, it's always a bit difficult to account for all variables that influences how much of it comes from genetics. In the case of intelligence, it's even more so difficult because of the complexity of the human brain. Although we don't yet have a complete picture of how the mind works, we can identify what factors influence intelligence. Let's take the story of Christopher Langan.

Christopher Langan, often called “the smartest man in America” with an IQ estimated between 195 and 210, showed signs of extraordinary intelligence from an early age—reading complex texts and mastering advanced subjects as a child. He had a perfect score on the SATs (preparing for it in his head during the test) and was known for teaching him self advanced subjects like physics, math and logic in elementary school.

Now the life circumstances for Chris were not favorable at all. Despite his remarkable mind, he faced a difficult upbringing marked by poverty and abuse, and dropped out of college due to financial and personal setbacks. Logically speaking, we can't argue that he had a support system around him that enabled him to be so smart. For a person that thought himself how to read from newspapers and radios, no one can be a better example for being born smart. We don't exactly know why Chris was born this way. Maybe something in his genetics unlocked his brain to use more of it's capacity than the average joe. The gap here is between his natural born intellect and him not finding much success in life. Why couldn't he translate his raw brain power into what we traditionally attribute as success?

## Nurture: The Power of Environment and Effort

Aristotle once said “Give me a child until he is 7 and I will show you the man”. He put heavy emphasis on bringing up a child the right way and that determining that's child's life from then on.

We as a society seem to put heavy emphasis on our children being good students but often forget our children need the right values and environment to excel academically and even in life. As we did with Christopher Langan, let's take a look into the life of J. Robert Oppenheimer.

J. Robert Oppenheimer, often called the "father of the atomic bomb," was a brilliant American physicist who led the Manhattan Project during World War II. Born in 1904 to a wealthy family in New York, he was a precocious child who excelled academically and developed a deep love for literature and science. He studied at Harvard, Cambridge, and Göttingen, becoming a prominent theoretical physicist Even though both Chris and Robert showed great promise when they were younger, one became a very famous and successful scientist while the other struggled to complete his university education. The funny thing is that Chris couldn't continue with his classes because of a broken down car while Robert tried poisoning his tutor in college but getting off with a slap on the wrist. Robert had the social skills and the reputation to survive such an incident because he was brought up to have other skills to complement his intellect and the knowledge of how to effectively apply his smarts. Who knows, if Chris had the same skills as Robert, he might have been a very different man had he completed his studies.

## Why Nurture Has the Bigger Edge

We have some evidence in recent decades that the global average IQ scores have been rising by 3 points per decade. This can largely be attributed to better living conditions, improved education system and more exposure to faster means of information intake like books and computers. While it can also be argued that we are somehow evolving to be smarter, the current evidence leans towards it being more environmental than genetic.

Although I can list hundreds of studies that suggest a person's environment when growing up has a much higher influence on how smart they are, there are other types of intelligence to consider about. Having good communication skills, self awareness and a sense of purpose are skills as important as intellect. If one is lower than the rest, the others can compensate to get a close enough outcome.

Having a good school, 2 parent household and good nutrition has more impact than a person being born with genius level IQ but not having the means to apply or even understand it. Living in this world, it's all about application of intellect combined with our other skills.

## Final Message

Nature gives some of us a head start. Some of us might find it difficult to perform some intellectual tasks. Consequently, the environment we grew up in also has a big influence on how smart we are and how successful we can be.

Worry not. What ever card you've been dealt, both by nature and by your environment, you have the power to change it. It might not be easy, it rarely is, but changing the factors that control your intelligence even as a grown adult will change your perspective on how capable you are on doing anything. It is you who has to take control of your brain and nurture it to reach it's full potential!

You might not control your starting line, but you can absolutely control your direction.
